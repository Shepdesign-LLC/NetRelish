01 · Jars

A jar is a project.

Not a bookmarks folder. A jar holds the client's site, their hosting login, the local copy you're building, and every note you've written about them. One click brings all of it back.

  • Name it, color it, decide how much it holds
  • It fills with relish as you add to it
  • When it's full, seal it — the project is finished and preserved
  • Drag jars, tabs and notes anywhere you want them

02 · Notes

Write it down where it belongs.

Every jar has its own notes. Not in another app, not in a text file on the desktop — right there with the tabs they're about. Type in the panel and hit keep, or open a full page and write properly.

  • Every note carries a timestamp and its jar's color
  • Right-click to rename, duplicate, move or archive
  • Archive always offers undo — nothing is ever really deleted
  • Drag them into whatever order makes sense to you

03 · Tabs

Pin what matters. Lock what you can't lose.

A tab is a website, and it lives in a jar. Pin the ones you're always in and they shrink to a favicon at the front. Lock a tab and its close button disappears entirely — no more killing the client's hosting dashboard at 11pm.

  • Right-click to pin, lock, copy the link or move it to another jar
  • Drag a tab onto a jar on the shelf to move it there
  • Tabs you stop using drift down to the brine instead of vanishing

04 · Dev mode

For people who build sites, not just read them.

Flip one switch and your local WordPress copies appear — inside the jar that owns them. Start a site, open it, back it up, push it live. Flip it back off and NetRelish is a normal browser again.

  • No terminal. No command line. Not once.
  • Mail from a local site is caught, so you never email a real customer by accident
  • Problems are written in plain English, not dumped as log files

Questions

The obvious ones.

What's a jar, really?

A project. It holds the tabs and the notes that go together — a client, a build you're working on, things you mean to read. Open the jar and every tab comes back the way you left it. That's the whole idea, and it's the only thing you need to learn.

Do I have to be a developer?

No. Dev mode is off by default and stays off unless you turn it on. With it off, NetRelish is a browser with notes. With it on, it's a browser that can also run your WordPress sites locally — still with no terminal.

Where does my stuff live?

On your Mac. Notes, jars, tabs and local sites are all local. Sync between machines is coming and will be optional; nothing is uploaded unless you ask for it.

Is this Chrome underneath?

WebKit — the same engine as Safari. That's why the glass and the animations feel native, and it's why it doesn't eat your battery.

What does it cost?

Free during the beta, and free after it for anyone in the beta. Pricing for everyone else gets decided with the testers, not before.

Why "relish"?

Because it's the thing you add that makes the rest work, and because jars were already the right metaphor. The pun came second, honestly.
